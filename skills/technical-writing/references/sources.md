# Sources and usage conditions

This document explains the roles and limitations of the sources used to
develop the skill. Its descriptions and examples are original editorial
guidance, not a reproduction of a standard or an official comparison.

## Comparison

| Source or evidence | Role in this skill | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ISO 24495-1 | Informs a reader-centered approach to whether information is relevant, findable, understandable, and usable. | Correct English usage, technical truth, complete accessibility, or conformance to another standard. |
| ASD-STE100 | Informs selected practices for reducing ambiguity and using technical terms consistently. | That general English writing follows the complete controlled language, its dictionary, or all its rules. |
| Dictionaries such as Merriam-Webster and Oxford Dictionaries | Help check spelling, definitions, and usage in the relevant English variety. | A complete house style, technical accuracy, or a single authority for all English varieties. |
| Regional public-sector guidance such as the US Federal Plain Language Guidelines, GOV.UK Style Guide, and Australian Style Manual | Offers practical plain-language and editorial guidance for its stated jurisdiction and audience. | A rulebook for every country, publisher, genre, or technical domain. |
| Publication and discipline guides such as Chicago, AP, APA, and MLA | Resolve style choices when a publisher, institution, or discipline specifies or adopts that guide. | Interchangeable rules or requirements for documents outside their relevant context. |
| Applicable English and project style guidance | Helps distinguish spelling, grammar, usage, terminology, valid variants, and local editorial choices. | That a style choice is a universal rule, a technical claim is correct, or readers can use the document successfully. |
| Product specifications and other subject-matter evidence | Establish the facts, constraints, terminology, and behavior a particular document describes. | That the resulting prose is clear, findable, accessible, or suitable for every audience. |
| W3C writing guidance | Helps with meaningful headings, links, instructions, and text alternatives where relevant. | Full accessibility or WCAG conformance from a prose review alone. |

Neither ISO 24495-1 nor ASD-STE100 alone covers all of this skill's goals.
ISO describes a reader-centered framework rather than an English spelling
guide or product specification. ASD-STE100 is an English controlled language
for technical documentation; this skill also supports general writing and
does not implement its complete rules or dictionary. English style guidance
and authoritative technical evidence are separate inputs, not substitutes for
either source.

Use the user's explicit instructions, applicable project rules, and the
existing document's context before choosing an editorial convention. For new
documents without a specified variant or house style, the skill defaults to
US English. The Google and Microsoft guides below are useful references for
technical communication, not mandatory house styles for every project.

Choose a reference for the question at hand: use a dictionary to check a word's
spelling or sense, a regional guide for conventions in its stated context, and
a publication or discipline guide when that guide applies to the deliverable.
Do not combine competing guides mechanically or elevate one publisher's
preference into a universal grammar rule. Follow the user's or project's
specified authority first.

Technical meaning remains a constraint across all these perspectives. Clear,
grammatical, well-organized prose can still be factually wrong. If a necessary
source is unavailable, state the limitation rather than claiming verification.

| [MLA Style Center](https://style.mla.org/) | Documentation and writing guidance for contexts that use MLA style. |

These references are background, not runtime dependencies. Look up a specific
source when its authority or details matter, and do not claim to have checked
an unavailable source.

## Source usage and rights

The repository's MIT license covers the skill's original content; it does not
change the rights or usage conditions of external publications. Public access,
free availability, reproduction, redistribution, and AI use are distinct
questions. Check each source's current conditions before reusing its content.
This summary is not legal advice.

### ASD-STE100

The [official use notice](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html)
describes the free official copy and reserves rights. It does not grant
individual permission or endorse third-party tools. The current edition's
copyright statement is the provider's stated reference for use conditions.

This skill uses independently written guidance informed by public
explanations. It does not bundle the standard, its controlled dictionary,
logos, or a reconstructed replacement. Free availability is not assumed to
authorize redistribution or a particular AI use. No affiliation, approval,
or certification by ASD or its maintenance group is claimed.

### ISO 24495-1

[ISO's copyright notice](https://www.iso.org/copyright.html) explains that ISO
online content and publications are protected and describes permission
requests. Access to a public introduction is not permission to redistribute
the standard. This skill does not include the full text or claim that its
complete provisions or a specific license have been reviewed.

### Other references

Google, Microsoft, W3C, and other publishers set conditions for their own
materials. Consult the applicable page and publisher terms before copying or
redistributing them; do not assume the repository's license applies to them.
Links and the skill's original explanations are not a replacement for their
source materials.

## Evaluation limits

The [evaluation cases](../evals/evals.json) contain proposed inputs and
grading criteria, not results. Deterministic structure checks cannot verify
model behavior, technical accuracy, accessibility, or reader comprehension.
Model-based and reader evaluations require separate authorization.
